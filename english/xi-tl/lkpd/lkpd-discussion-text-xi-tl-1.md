## **C. SPEAKING**

**Target**: Conduct a respectful pair debate/dialogue applying contrastive connectors, hedging expressions, and the KBC value of Tasamuh (Tolerance).

### **Speaking Scenario: "Digital Detox vs. Constant Connectivity"**

Work in pairs. Student A supports **"Mandatory Weekend Digital Detox for Madrasah Students"**, while Student B supports **"Unrestricted Weekend Digital Connectivity for Collaborative Projects"**.

### **Dialogue Framework & Useful Expressions**

- **Expressing Proponent View**: *"From my perspective, implementing a weekend digital detox allows students to..."*
- **Expressing Opponent View**: *"While I understand that point, on the other hand, constant connectivity enables..."*
- **Applying Hedging**: *"Evidence suggests that excessive screen time might potentially cause fatigue, whereas..."*
- **Demonstrating Tasamuh**: *"I respect your valid point regarding... however, we must also consider..."*

### **Speaking Prompt Instructions**

1. Practice a 3-minute dialogue with your partner ensuring both sides present at least 2 structured arguments.
2. Incorporate at least **two contrastive conjunctions** (*however, on the other hand, nevertheless*) and **two hedging expressions** (*evidence suggests, potentially, it appears that*).
3. Conclude with a mutually agreed moderate solution (Tawasuth).

**Assessment Criteria**: Fluency (25%), Pronunciation & Intonation (25%), Correct use of Connectors & Hedging (25%), Demonstration of Tasamuh & Respectful Communication (25%).

## **D. READING**

**Target**: Analyze structural components, infer implicit meanings, and evaluate KBC character values in an authentic Discussion Text.

### **Reading Text**

### **Social Media Algorithmic Echo Chambers: Connectivity or Division?**

Modern social media platforms are designed to maximize user engagement through complex recommendation algorithms. These algorithms continuously analyze user preferences, displaying content that aligns with their existing beliefs. While tech developers promote this feature as personalized connectivity, sociologists and educators debate whether algorithmic curation unites global communities or dangerously deepens societal polarization.

Proponents of personalized algorithms argue that tailored content feeds enhance user experience by delivering relevant news, educational materials, and cultural connections efficiently. Evidence suggests that algorithms help niche communities—such as young scientists, environmental activists, and language learners—find like-minded peers across geographical boundaries. Furthermore, supporters contend that algorithm-driven platforms empower marginalized voices by amplifying content that might otherwise be overlooked by traditional mainstream media.

Conversely, critics raise alarming concerns about the creation of "echo chambers" and filter bubbles. Opponents assert that when algorithms repeatedly feed users content that reinforces their biases, individuals become isolated from opposing viewpoints. Consequently, this cognitive isolation potentially fosters dogmatism, intolerance, and social division. Studies indicate that algorithmic echo chambers can accelerate the spread of misinformation, as sensationalized content tends to generate higher emotional reactions than nuanced, fact-checked reporting.

In conclusion, addressing the dilemma of algorithmic echo chambers requires a balanced, moderate approach (Tawasuth). While algorithm technology offers unprecedented access to tailored knowledge, digital citizens must actively cultivate Tasamuh (tolerance) and critical thinking. Rather than passively consuming algorithmically fed media, users should consciously seek diverse perspectives, verify information with intellectual integrity, and engage in compassionate dialogue with those holding different views.
