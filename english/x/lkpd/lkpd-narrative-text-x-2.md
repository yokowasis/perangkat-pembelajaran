## **C. SPEAKING**

**Target**: Perform a short role-play or oral storytelling showcasing an alternative story ending with a message of forgiveness and reconciliation.

### **Speaking Activity 1: Short Role-Play Performance**

Work in groups of 3. Perform a 3-minute mini drama based on the alternative ending of **"The Dragon and Ayla"**:

**Characters:**
- **Ayla** (Compassionate young girl)
- **The Dragon** (Pain-stricken creature turning peaceful)
- **Village Chief** (Initially fearful, learning tolerance and forgiveness)

**Sample Script Excerpt:**
> **Village Chief** : *(Scared)* "Ayla! Stay back! The beast will destroy us all!"  
> **Ayla** : *(Calmly)* "Chief, please wait! He is not evil; he is in pain. Look at his swollen paw!"  
> **Dragon** : *(Groaning softly)* "Grrr... help me... it hurts so much..."  
> **Ayla** : *(Gently removing the thorn)* "Hold still... there! You are free now."  
> **Dragon** : *(Relieved)* "Thank you, little human. I promise to protect your crops forever."  
> **Village Chief** : *(Touched)* "Forgive me, Dragon. We judged you without understanding. Welcome to our village!"  

---

### **Speaking Activity 2: Storyteller's Reflection**

After performing, each student shares 1 sentence in English explaining what their character felt during the moment of forgiveness.

**Speaking Rubric & Focus:**
- Fluency & Confidence (smooth delivery with clear voice).
- Pronunciation & Intonation (expressing pain, relief, and joy).
- KBC Value Integration (conveying *tasamuh*, forgiveness, and peacemaking).

**Assessment**: Students must perform the role-play with acceptable pronunciation, good emotional expression, and clear delivery of the reconciliation message.

## **D. READING**

**Target**: Analyze complex narrative text, evaluate character mindset transformation, and reflect on restorative reconciliation (*Cinta Diri & Sesama*).

### **Reading Text**

**The Wolf and the Shepherds of Mount Tambora**

For many years, the sheep farmers of Mount Tambora lived in constant fear of a solitary grey wolf named Kuro. Whenever livestock went missing, the angry villagers gathered with torches and hunting dogs, swearing to eliminate Kuro. Kuro was painted in local folklore as a bloodthirsty monster that deserved no mercy.

One freezing evening, a young shepherd named Tariq was searching for a stray lamb near the rocky ridge. In a dark cave, he discovered Kuro trapped under a heavy fallen boulder. The wolf's rear leg was pinned, and he was shivering from exhaustion and hunger. Instead of attacking Tariq, the wolf looked into the boy's eyes with whimpering sorrow and utter helplessness.

Tariq remembered his teacher's words at the madrasah: *"Allah created every animal with purpose, and true bravery lies in showing mercy to the defenseless."* Taking a deep breath, Tariq used a sturdy wooden log as a lever. With all his strength, he lifted the heavy rock. Kuro crawled out slowly, his leg limping, but his eyes glowing with quiet gratitude. Tariq shared his loaf of bread and wrapped the wolf's injured leg with clean cloth.

The next morning, Tariq led Kuro back to the village. The shepherds raised their spears in alarm, but Tariq stepped in front of the wolf, saying, "Stop! Kuro did not attack our sheep out of evilness; he was starving because his pack had vanished. He needed help, not hatred." 

Touched by Tariq's courage and seeing the wolf gently resting at the boy's feet, the village elders lowered their weapons. They agreed to leave leftover meat at the forest edge so Kuro would not starve. In return, Kuro guarded the village boundary from wild predators. The ancient feud ended not with bloodshed, but with an unexpected bond of brotherhood between man and nature.

---

### **Comprehension Questions**

1. **Conflict Analysis**: What was the long-standing conflict between the shepherds of Mount Tambora and Kuro the wolf?
2. **Key Discovery**: What did Tariq discover in the dark cave, and how did Kuro look at him?
3. **Moral Motivation**: What lesson from the madrasah inspired Tariq to help the trapped wolf instead of harming him?
4. **Mindset Transformation**: How did Tariq convince the village elders to lower their weapons and change their perspective on Kuro?
5. **KBC Reflection**: How does this story exemplify the principle of *Compassionate Communication* (koneksi sebelum koreksi) and *Ukhuwah Insaniyah* towards all living creatures?

**Assessment**: Students must answer at least 4 out of 5 questions correctly in complete, coherent sentences.

## **E. WRITING**

**Target**: Write an Alternative Ending (paragraf resolusi) for a classic fairytale/fable from a Compassion & Reconciliation POV.

### **Writing Task: Re-imagining a Classic Story Ending**

Choose **ONE** classic story below and write an alternative ending (2 paragraphs, 8-10 sentences) in English where the conflict is resolved through forgiveness, dialogue, and restoration instead of violence or punishment:

- **Option A**: *The Three Little Pigs* (The pigs invite the hungry wolf for warm soup and build him a house).
- **Option B**: *Cinderella and the Stepsisters* (Cinderella forgives her stepsisters and invites them to work together in the palace).
- **Option C**: *The Boy Who Cried Wolf* (The villagers help the boy learn honesty through gentle mentorship instead of abandoning him).

---

### **Writing Guidelines & Steps**

1. **Paragraph 1 - The Turning Point (Mindset Change)**:  
   Describe how the protagonist chooses to offer help or forgiveness instead of punishment. Use *past tense* and *direct speech*.  
   *Example*: *"Cinderella looked into her stepsisters' tearful eyes and said gently, 'I forgive you both from the bottom of my heart.'"*

2. **Paragraph 2 - The Restorative Resolution (Peaceful Outcome)**:  
   Describe how both sides work together to restore peace and live in harmony (*Cinta Diri & Sesama*).  
   *Example*: *"From that day on, they managed the kingdom's charitable projects together, proving that love heals all wounds."*

---

### **Writing Checklist & Rubric Target**
- [ ] Uses simple past tense (*forgave, invited, spoke, changed*).
- [ ] Includes at least 1 direct speech sentence with proper quotation marks (`"..."`).
- [ ] Integrates vocabulary of reconciliation (*forgiveness, dialog, peace, compassion*).
- [ ] Ends with a clear KBC moral reflection.

**Assessment**: Students must submit a complete 2-paragraph alternative ending meeting all checklist criteria with less than 4 grammar/spelling errors.
