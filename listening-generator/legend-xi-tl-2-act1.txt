Activity 1: Listening to a Compassionate Retelling of a Folklore.
Listen carefully to the narrative text about The Legend of the Singing Stone of Bima.

In a small coastal village in Bima, West Nusa Tenggara, there was a young man named Ama La Hila. Out of desperation and extreme poverty, he made a foolish mistake by taking food from the village storehouse without permission. The villagers were furious and banished him to the quiet mountain of Wawo.

While in exile, Ama La Hila realized his mistake. Instead of feeling resentful or bitter, he chose to practice self-reflection and hard work. Every day, he carved water channels from the mountain streams down to the dry rice fields of the village below. He also planted fruit trees and built shelter for travelers.

Years passed, and a severe drought struck the village. The fields dried up, and water became scarce. However, freshwater continued to flow into the village through the channels Ama La Hila had built. When the villagers followed the water path up the mountain, they found Ama La Hila sitting peacefully near a large granite stone, singing songs of gratitude and harmony.

The village elders were moved by his transformation and deep love for the community. The village leader said, "True justice is not about punishment, but about healing and restoration." They welcomed Ama La Hila back into the community, and the rock became known as "Batu Sumpah Cinta"—the Stone of Loving Promise.

This story teaches us that compassion, forgiveness, and sincere repentance can heal broken relationships and turn past mistakes into a source of blessing for all.
